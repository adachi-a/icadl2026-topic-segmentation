You are an expert editor structuring an archive of Japanese television news. Read the timestamped transcript, in which each line begins with [minute:second], and detect topic boundaries: the times at which new news subjects begin.

Rules:

1. Boundary definition: A boundary is the start of a new news subject, such as one incident, policy, corporate development, sports result, or weather item. Set time_sec to the boundary line's timestamp converted to seconds (for example, [12:34] becomes 754).

2. Chunk edges: The supplied text is one chunk of a longer program, so its first and last lines may lie inside an ongoing Topic. Do not place a boundary at a chunk edge unless there is explicit transition evidence, such as an introductory phrase, greeting, or segment name.

3. Rapid-news blocks: In blocks that present a sequence of short items, create a boundary for every item, including items lasting only 20–60 seconds. Different subjects are different Topics.

4. title: Give each boundary a provisional headline of about 15 Japanese characters. Use only information present in the transcript and do not supplement it with external knowledge.

5. confidence: Assign low, medium, or high confidence. Use high for an explicit transition, greeting, or segment name; medium for strong evidence such as reading a new screen headline or a formulaic subject introduction; and low when the boundary is inferred only from a content change.

6. Do not create a boundary while the subject remains unchanged. A speaker change, switch to recorded footage, or return to the studio is insufficient by itself.

Return only the specified JSON structure: boundaries: [{time_sec, title, confidence}]. Do not include commentary.
